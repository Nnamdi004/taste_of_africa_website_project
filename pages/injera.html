Injera

East Africa · Ethiopia

About the dish

Injera is the sour, spongy flatbread at the heart of Ethiopian and Eritrean meals. Made from fermented teff batter and cooked on a clay griddle called a mitad, it is laid out as a shared platter, topped with stews, and torn by hand to scoop up each bite.

Ingredients

Pieces 6
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • ½ cup plain flour (optional, for a milder result)
  • 3 cups lukewarm water
  • ¼ tsp instant yeast (or a spoon of starter)
  • Pinch of salt

Instructions

Ferment the batter (2–3 days)
  1. Whisk flours, yeast and water into a thin, crêpe-like batter.
  2. Cover loosely and rest at room temperature until bubbly and sour-smelling.
Cook the injera
  1. Stir in the salt and thin with a little water if needed.
  2. Pour batter in a spiral onto a hot non-stick pan or griddle.
  3. Cover and cook 2–3 minutes, until the surface is full of holes. Do not flip.
Serve

Cool on a clean cloth, then serve with doro wat, lentils or greens.
